01 — Strategy Before Content

Why You're Marketing + What Success Looks Like

Starting from zero on social doesn't mean starting with nothing to say. It means every post has a job. Before you write a single caption, you need to know what each platform is actually for — because the mistake most startups make is treating all channels the same.

The Two-Audience Rule

Every piece of content you create does one of two things: it builds trust with parents who are your direct users, or it builds credibility with attorneys who are your acquisition channel. When an attorney googles you before a demo, they'll find your social. When they show their paralegal, they'll scroll your Instagram. Your social presence is a silent sales tool for the attorney-first model — not just a parent acquisition channel. Design it for both audiences even when you're talking to one.

What Social Does for Parents

  • Builds awareness — "I've seen this before" removes purchase friction
  • Provides free value (co-parenting tips) — earns trust before asking for signup
  • Normalizes using a platform — "5,000 other parents use this, it must work"
  • Captures organic traffic from people actively searching co-parenting content
  • Creates a retargeting audience for when you do run paid ads

What Social Does for Attorneys

  • Credibility check — attorney googles CommonGround before responding to your cold email
  • Social proof — "they have 2,000 followers, real families use this"
  • Shows brand quality — professional content signals you're not a fly-by-night startup
  • LinkedIn (add this!) is where attorney referrals and warm intros happen
  • A case study post is a sales asset the attorney can share with firm partners

What Social Does NOT Do

  • Directly generate attorney sales — cold social rarely converts B2B
  • Replace email outreach — attorneys don't DM you from Instagram
  • Build itself — you have to show up every week, not just launch and forget
  • Work without a clear CTA — engagement without signup links is vanity
  • Substitute for a working product — social amplifies what's real, it doesn't create it
★

Add LinkedIn now. You have Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok — all parent-focused. You have zero attorney-focused organic channels. LinkedIn is where family law attorneys spend professional time, share articles, and get peer recommendations. A CommonGround LinkedIn page posting attorney-focused content (case efficiency, legal tech, ARIA intake results) directly feeds your cold outreach — attorneys who see your content before your email reply at 3x the rate. Set it up this week alongside everything else.

03 — Content Types That Work

The 8 Post Formats — With Examples

Vary these across the week. Never post the same type twice in a row. Each one serves a different stage of the parent's journey from "never heard of CommonGround" to "paying subscriber."

💡

Type 1 — Co-Parenting Tips (Awareness)

Practical, shareable advice about communication, scheduling, and reducing conflict. No pitch. Pure value. This is how strangers find you. Example: "5 things to never say to your co-parent (and what to say instead)." Target: saves and shares.

🔄

Type 2 — Before / After (ARIA Transformation)

Show a hostile message on the left, ARIA's rewrite on the right. This is your most powerful demo content because it's instantly understandable. Viewers see the product working in 3 seconds. Example: [hostile: "You always forget to pick him up"] → [ARIA: "I noticed [child] wasn't picked up at the usual time. Can we discuss the schedule?"]. Target: saves, follows, signups.

👦

Type 3 — Child-Centered Facts (Emotional)

Research-backed stats about how conflict affects children. Not preachy — empathetic and informative. Example: "Children who witness frequent co-parent conflict are 3x more likely to show anxiety symptoms. Here's what helps." Lead with child impact, land on the solution. Target: shares, comments, emotional resonance.

❓

Type 4 — Question / Poll (Engagement)

Ask your audience a question that reveals their pain point. Example: "What's the most frustrating part of co-parenting? A) Scheduling conflicts B) Financial disputes C) Communication breakdowns D) Lack of documentation." The answers are your product roadmap AND your ad targeting data. Target: comments, engagement rate.

📱

Type 5 — Product Demo (Conversion)

Screen recording of the actual app — 30-60 seconds. Show one specific feature in action. Not a full tour. Example: "This is how ARIA rewrites a message in real time." "This is how you log a custody exchange in 10 seconds." "This is what a court-ready report looks like." Target: link-in-bio clicks, signups.

📊

Type 6 — Statistics / Data (Credibility)

One stat, big visual, short explanation. Example: "Families who use structured co-parenting communication reduce court modification filings by up to 50%." Source everything. Frame as education, not marketing. This content gets shared by therapists, attorneys, and child advocates — your best unpaid distribution channels. Target: shares to professional audiences.

💬

Type 7 — Testimonial / Story (Trust)

Real story, real parent, real result. Even at zero users, you can use the storytelling framework: write a realistic composite story about "Sarah" that reflects real pain points (without naming a real person). As actual users join, get their permission and use their words. Target: comments, DMs asking "how do I get this."

⚖️

Type 8 — Attorney / Legal Angle (Credibility + B2B)

Content that positions CommonGround as what attorneys already recommend. Example: "3 things family law attorneys wish their clients would do from day one." "Why family law attorneys are now requiring co-parenting apps for high-conflict cases." This builds credibility with parents AND gets shared by attorneys to their networks. Target: shares by professionals, attorney DMs.

The most important month 1 task that nobody does: Engage with every comment on every post. Every single one, every platform. Reply within 2 hours if possible. When you have 0–200 followers, this 1:1 engagement is what the algorithm rewards and what converts scrollers into followers. After 1,000 followers you can scale back. In the first month it's mandatory.

10 — What to Measure

The Only Metrics That Matter When You're Starting Out

Don't spend more than 30 minutes per week on analytics in month one. You don't have enough data for insights. Watch a few leading indicators and react to them. Save the deep analysis for month 3 when you have enough data to see patterns.

The One Number That Tells You If Marketing Is Working

At month 3, look at this: of the attorneys who had a demo with you, how many had seen CommonGround on social before you reached out? If it's >30%, your social presence is doing its job as a credibility layer for your cold outreach. If it's 0%, your attorney content (LinkedIn) needs more volume. That single question connects your social investment directly to your revenue model.
