01 — Why This Feature

The Gap in the Current Platform

Right now, co-parents communicate through text messages on CommonGround. ARIA mediates those messages before they're sent. But when parents need to have a real conversation — about a school decision, a medical situation, a schedule change that's complicated — they're leaving the platform to call or FaceTime, and everything that happens in that call is unmonitored, undocumented, and legally invisible.

The Problem Today

  • Co-parents leave the platform for voice/video calls — all that communication disappears from the record
  • Threats, harassment, and hostile language in calls has zero documentation — no ARIA, no log, no court evidence
  • Attorneys have no visibility into what actually happened on a call their client reported as hostile
  • DV survivors have no protected channel for necessary communication — they're using unmonitored calls
  • Both parents can claim the other "said something terrible on that call" with zero way to verify either side

What Co-Parent Calling Solves

  • Every call on the platform is monitored by ARIA — audio and video in real time
  • ARIA flags hostile language, threats, profanity — with timestamps and transcripts for each flag
  • Attorneys can see a call log with ARIA flag summary — without hearing the call themselves
  • Court-admissible call record: duration, participants, flag count, severity distribution, SHA-256
  • DV survivors have a monitored channel where harassment is automatically detected and logged
  • ARIA coaching mode: real-time text suggestions during a call when language is escalating
02 — Consent Framework

Recording Consent — This Has to Be Right

⚖ Two-Party Consent Laws Apply to This Feature

Recording a phone or video call requires the consent of all parties in most US states — including California, the primary launch market. The platform's Terms of Service must explicitly disclose that all calls made through CommonGround are recorded and monitored. But ToS acceptance alone is not enough for every jurisdiction. You need active, in-the-moment consent before every call connects. This is non-negotiable and must be designed before a single line of call code is written.

The Three-Layer Consent Model

  • Layer 1 — Terms of Service (Account Creation): Both parents agree at signup that all CommonGround communications — including calls — are recorded, monitored by ARIA, and may be used as court evidence. This is a blanket consent covering all future calls.
  • Layer 2 — Feature Consent Screen (First Call Ever): Before a parent can make or receive their first call, they see a dedicated consent screen that clearly states "All calls on CommonGround are recorded and monitored by ARIA for compliance purposes. Both parties are aware of and have consented to this recording." They must tap "I Understand and Consent" — not just dismiss it. This is stored as a consent_event in the database with timestamp.
  • Layer 3 — Pre-Call Notice (Every Call): Both the initiating parent and receiving parent see a brief banner before every call connects: "This call is recorded and monitored. Both parties have consented." The receiving parent's call answer screen displays this notice prominently. The call cannot connect until the receiving parent answers — which constitutes their ongoing consent by continued platform use.

Every consent event must be stored in a dedicated table — not just in the ToS acceptance record. parent_call_consents stores: user_id, consent_type ('tos_acceptance' | 'feature_screen' | 'call_answer'), timestamp, IP address, app version. When a court or attorney asks "did both parties know this call was being recorded?" — you produce this table entry. It is SHA-256 hashed like all audit records.

03 — Feature Specification

Co-Parent Calling — What It Is and What It Does

Voice-only on Plus. Video + Voice on Complete. All calls monitored by ARIA regardless of tier. The feature is additive to messaging — it doesn't replace it. Some conversations need to be text. Some need a real-time voice. The platform now supports both with the same documentation and monitoring standards.

Call Types + Tier Access

  • Voice Only — Plus tier ($17.99/mo): Audio call between co-parents. ARIA audio monitoring active. No video. Recorded. SHA-256 on call record.
  • Video + Voice — Complete tier ($34.99/mo): Full video call. Both ARIA audio AND video monitoring active. Recorded. SHA-256 on call record.
  • Initiating: Either parent can initiate a call at any time (no time window restrictions like KidSpace — adults manage their own schedules)
  • Declining is always available: The receiving parent can always decline. There is no way to force a co-parent to answer. Declined calls are logged (time, who declined) but not as a compliance violation.
  • Call duration limits: No hard limit, but attorney can set a recommended duration in the Professional Portal (shows as a soft reminder at that time — not a termination)
  • Both participants see the same recording notice banner throughout the call — a small persistent indicator at the top of the call screen: "🔴 Recording"

In-Call Features

  • ARIA Coaching Sidebar (optional): A small overlay on the side of the call screen that shows ARIA's real-time assessment of the conversation tone. Color changes from green → yellow → orange as hostility escalates. Parent can collapse it. The co-parent cannot see this sidebar — it's personal to each participant.
  • Topic anchoring: Before or during a call, either parent can set a "call topic" — "Discussing Emma's school enrollment." This gets associated with the call record. Helps attorneys understand call context without listening to it.
  • Leave a note: After a call ends, either parent can add a brief note (up to 300 chars) summarizing what was decided. The note is attached to the call log. Both parents can see both notes. ARIA moderates these notes.
  • Quick Accord from call: If an agreement is reached on a call, parent can immediately tap "Create Quick Accord" from the post-call screen — pre-filled with the call topic if one was set.
  • Mute: Standard mute functionality. Mute status is logged — if a parent mutes during a flagged period, that's noted in the call record.
  • No in-call text chat between co-parents during a call — this prevents side-channel communication that could circumvent the call monitoring. If they want to share something text-based, they send a message separately.

What Both Parents See

  • Each other's name and avatar
  • Call duration timer
  • Recording indicator (always visible)
  • Topic label if one was set
  • Mute / end call controls
  • Video on/off toggle (Complete tier)

What Only Each Parent Sees (Private)

  • Their own ARIA Coaching Sidebar (tone indicator)
  • Their own post-call note field
  • Suggestions if ARIA detects escalation on their end
  • The co-parent never sees your ARIA sidebar or suggestions

What Neither Parent Sees

  • The full ARIA flag log during the call
  • The call transcript in real time
  • The co-parent's ARIA sidebar
  • The evidence record if a call segment is flagged
  • Post-call: both can see the call's flag summary count — not the transcript or scores
04 — New ARIA Mode: co_parent_call

How This Mode Differs From Parent Messaging and KidSpace

Co-parent calling needs its own ARIA mode — distinct from both the text messaging pipeline (pre-send analysis) and KidSpace Guard (child-focused, extra-sensitive). Adult co-parent calls have different dynamics, different thresholds, and a different available action set. You cannot auto-terminate an adult call the way you can a KidSpace session.

⚡

The key difference from KidSpace: ARIA cannot unilaterally end a co-parent call. These are adults. Terminating their call is an interference with their communication that could have legal implications — particularly in cases where courts have ordered co-parents to communicate. ARIA can flag, escalate, send real-time notifications, and provide strong coaching signals — but the actual call termination is always the parent's choice or requires explicit attorney intervention.

Pythonaria/rules/decision_engine.py — Add co_parent_call to THRESHOLDS
THRESHOLDS = { 'parent': { # text messaging — pre-send 'suggest': 0.35, 'flag': 0.60, 'block': 0.85, }, 'kidspace': { # child communications — extra sensitive 'suggest': 0.20, 'flag': 0.40, 'hold': 0.55, 'block': 0.70, 'end_session': 0.80, }, 'co_parent_call': { # NEW — adult co-parent voice/video calls 'coach': 0.30, # show coaching sidebar warning (yellow) 'coach_strong': 0.50, # coaching sidebar turns orange — strong warning 'flag': 0.65, # log flag + notify attorney if linked 'flag_urgent': 0.80, # flag + notify attorney immediately + alert both parents post-call 'threat_detected': 0.85, # special: threat language → immediate alert # NOTE: No 'end_session' in this mode — adults cannot be force-terminated # No 'block' — can't block real-time speech # No 'hold' — can't hold speech in transit } } # Available actions for co_parent_call mode: # - allow → content within normal bounds, log only # - coach → update the caller's ARIA sidebar indicator (only they see it) # - flag → create a timed event record, notify attorney post-call # - flag_urgent → create event record + send push notification to attorney NOW # - threat_alert → immediate push to both parents and attorney, create evidence record

ARIA Coaching Sidebar — Real-Time Tone Signal

Each parent sees their own private tone indicator in the corner of the call screen. It reflects ARIA's assessment of the last 10–15 seconds of their speech specifically — not the whole call, not the other parent.

  • 🟢 Green — "Communicating well": Tone is constructive. No intervention.
  • 🟡 Yellow — "Tension detected": ARIA score ≥ 0.30. Sidebar shows a gentle nudge: "Consider pausing to listen." Nothing more.
  • 🟠 Orange — "Escalating": Score ≥ 0.50. Sidebar: "This conversation is becoming heated. Take a breath before responding." Parent sees this, co-parent does not.
  • 🔴 Red — "High conflict": Score ≥ 0.65. Sidebar: "This call is being flagged. Consider ending and continuing in writing." A flag event is written to the record.
  • The sidebar is opt-out by default. Parent can collapse it in settings. The flag still occurs at the threshold regardless of whether the sidebar is visible.

What Constitutes a Flag in co_parent_call Mode

  • Hostility score ≥ 0.65 for a sustained 10-second window
  • Profanity score ≥ 0.70 in any 10-second window
  • Threat language detection ≥ 0.80 (any credible threat, regardless of duration)
  • Controlling language pattern ≥ 0.75 sustained over 30 seconds
  • Distress signal from the receiving parent ≥ 0.60 (crying, fear, requests for help — same detection as KidSpace audio)
  • A "flag" in this mode creates a coparent_call_events record with: timestamp, flag_type, severity, score, transcript_excerpt (10s window), which parent triggered it
  • Flag does NOT interrupt the call. Flag does NOT notify the other parent during the call. Both parents receive a post-call summary if any flags occurred.
05 — Video Monitoring Pipeline

AWS Rekognition on Co-Parent Video Calls

Video monitoring applies to Complete tier calls only (video is not available on Plus). The pipeline is identical to KidSpace's video analysis but with different action thresholds — because these are adults and the platform cannot auto-terminate their calls.

What Gets Scanned and At What Rate

  • Frame rate: 1 frame every 10 seconds during normal call · Escalates to every 3 seconds if a prior video flag occurred in the last 120 seconds
  • Rekognition labels checked: Nudity · Explicit Sexual Activity · Violence · Weapons · Drugs · Hate Symbols
  • Confidence threshold: Any moderation label with confidence ≥ 75% triggers evaluation
  • Note: Unlike KidSpace, we do NOT check for "Suggestive" or "Rude Gestures" at the same threshold — adults have different social norms. Threshold for those categories is ≥ 90%.
  • Nudity ≥ 85%: Flag event created + attorney notified + both parents receive a post-call notification that a video compliance event occurred. Call is NOT terminated automatically.
  • Weapons ≥ 80%: Flag event + immediate push notification to attorney if linked. If no attorney, notification goes to admin for manual review.
  • Hate Symbol ≥ 75%: Flag event + attorney notification.
  • Violence ≥ 85%: Flag event + attorney + admin alert.
  • Video evidence frame is extracted and SHA-256 hashed when a flag occurs. The frame is stored as an evidence artifact — not accessible to either parent, only to attorneys and admin through formal process.
→

Why we don't auto-terminate adult co-parent calls on video flags: A co-parent could deliberately hold up a knife, trigger an auto-termination, and then claim the platform "cut off their legal right to communicate with the other parent." Courts have ordered many co-parents to communicate. Terminating that communication unilaterally creates liability. Instead, the flag is recorded with full evidence, the attorney is notified immediately, and the attorney makes the decision about whether to advise their client to end the call. The platform protects through documentation, not interruption.

06 — Audio Monitoring Pipeline

Real-Time Transcription + ARIA Classification

The audio pipeline runs on all co-parent calls — both Plus (voice-only) and Complete (video + voice). AssemblyAI handles real-time transcription. Claude/OpenAI handles classification of each 10-second transcript window. The rules engine handles the decision. Same architecture as KidSpace audio, different mode config.

Step 1
Audio Capture
Daily.co sends audio stream to backend WebSocket endpoint. Two separate streams — one per participant.
Step 2
AssemblyAI Transcription
Streaming transcription. Produces rolling 10-second transcript windows per participant. Speaker-labeled.
Step 3
ARIA Classification
Each 10s window sent to Claude (primary) / OpenAI (fallback) with co_parent_call prompt. Returns JSON scores.
Step 4
Rules Engine Decides
decide(analysis, mode='co_parent_call', history). Returns ARIADecision: allow / coach / flag / flag_urgent / threat_alert.
Step 5
Action Executed
Update caller's coaching sidebar in real time via Supabase Realtime. Write flag event to DB. Notify attorney if threshold met.
Step 6
Post-Call
Full transcript stored. Call summary generated. Both parents receive summary if flags occurred. Attorney gets summary always.

08 — Attorney Portal Integration

How Co-Parent Calls Surface in the Professional Portal

The attorney portal's Calls & Logs tab already exists in the Client Communication section. It currently shows KidSpace call logs. Co-parent calls are added to the same tab with a clear visual distinction — attorneys can see both children's calls and co-parent calls from one screen.

What Attorneys See Per Call

  • Call date, start time, duration
  • Who initiated the call
  • Call topic if one was set by either parent
  • Total ARIA flag count and severity distribution (e.g., "2 events: 1 medium, 1 high")
  • Flag types that occurred — not the transcript, just the categories: "Hostility, Threat language"
  • Transcript excerpt for flagged windows (severity 3+) — attorney can read the 10-second window that triggered the flag
  • Post-call notes added by either parent (both visible to attorney)
  • Any Quick Accords created from the call
  • SHA-256 hash of the call record
  • Download call log as PDF (SHA-256 verified — court-submittable)

What Attorneys Do NOT See

  • Full call transcript (only flagged 10-second windows)
  • Audio or video recording of the call
  • Non-flagged portions of the transcript
  • The ARIA scores themselves (only the flag categories)
  • Which parent's ARIA sidebar showed what
  • The full transcript and recording are available through a separate legal evidence request process — attorney submits a formal request, CommonGround legal reviews, and if appropriate the full transcript is provided with proper chain of custody documentation. This is not a portal self-serve action.

09 — DV Safety Considerations

High-Conflict and Domestic Violence Case Handling

Co-parent calling for DV-flagged cases requires special handling. The platform cannot force a DV survivor to call their abuser — but it can provide the safest possible environment if they choose to do so, and it can detect the warning signs that attorneys and advocates need to see.

DV Flag Modifications to Call Behavior

  • DV flag is set by attorney or on case creation: When a case is marked as DV-involved, co-parent call thresholds tighten automatically — same as KidSpace's extra-sensitive mode applied to the adult thresholds.
  • DV case threshold changes: All thresholds drop by 0.15. Flag fires at 0.50 instead of 0.65. Urgent flag at 0.65 instead of 0.80. Distress signal threshold drops to 0.45.
  • Distress signal on DV case: Any distress signal ≥ 0.45 triggers an immediate push to the attorney and admin — not just a post-call summary. This is the one case where the monitoring system activates during the call for the survivor's benefit.
  • Threat detection on DV case: Any threat_score ≥ 0.70 (vs. 0.85 standard) triggers the receiving parent's safety banner immediately.
  • Calls are optional for DV cases — NEVER mandatory. The platform never coerces contact between a DV survivor and their abuser. Silent Handoff and text messaging are always available as non-contact alternatives.

Survivor-Protective Features During Calls

  • Quick End Call button: Large, prominent "End Call" button always visible on DV-flagged cases — never minimized or hidden by other UI elements.
  • One-tap block after call: After any call ends on a DV case, receiving parent has a one-tap "Block future calls — send to messaging only" option that immediately prevents future voice/video calls from that co-parent. Requires their attorney confirmation to reverse.
  • Emergency contact display: During a DV-flagged call, a small persistent button shows the user's emergency contact (from My Circle) — one tap to call them immediately if needed.
  • Post-call safety check: 30 minutes after a DV-flagged call, the platform sends a check-in notification: "How are you doing after that call? If you need support, [tap here]." Links to crisis resources.
  • All DV case call records automatically flagged for attorney review even if no ARIA event occurred — attorneys see every call on DV cases, not just flagged ones.

What This Feature Means for the Platform

Text messages capture a lot of co-parent conflict. Calls capture the rest — and historically that rest was invisible. The most serious harassment, the most credible threats, and the most traumatic interactions often happen on phone calls precisely because parents know those calls aren't being logged. Co-parent calling on CommonGround closes that gap. Every call becomes part of the documented co-parenting record. ARIA running in real-time means attorneys get a live picture of the communication pattern, not just what parents choose to report. And for DV survivors, for the first time, there's a monitored, documented, protected channel for necessary communication with someone they're required by courts to co-parent with.
