When a family law attorney says "use CommonGround," their client doesn't evaluate it. They use it. The trust transfer from attorney to platform is instantaneous. That's not true of any other referral source — not a nonprofit, not a Google ad, not a friend recommendation. Attorneys are your highest-trust, highest-activation, highest-LTV acquisition channel. Build around that reality.

Yellow flag: "I'd need to walk them through it myself." This attorney wants control — you'll need to show them how little effort is required.

Champion answer: "Absolutely — if it actually works, I'd tell every client to use it." This is who you want. Make them your pilot attorney.

Passive user answer: "Maybe, it depends on the case." This attorney will use it reactively, not proactively. They'll bring in 3 clients over 6 months instead of 30.

The difference in outcome: A champion attorney brings you 15–30 clients per year. A passive user brings you 3–5. Your entire model depends on finding the champions first.

What Makes This Email Work

  • Sent FROM the attorney, not from a company they've never heard of — trust transfer is immediate
  • One action: click the button. No account setup before clicking, no password creation gatekeeping
  • Free tier explicitly stated — removes cost anxiety before they even get to the platform
  • Co-parent asymmetry explained upfront — clients won't spend the first 10 minutes worrying about whether their ex needs to pay
  • Human support offered — "reply to this email" signals this isn't an automated dead end

What Will Kill the Flow (Fix These First)

  • Invitation email from a generic "@commonground.com" address, not attorney-branded — clients won't recognize it and won't open it
  • Account creation requires email verification before accessing anything — every extra step loses 20% of users
  • Onboarding requires co-parent to also sign up before client can do anything — this is a deadlock, must be avoidable
  • No status update to attorney when client activates — attorney assumes nobody joined and gives up
  • Attorney has to follow up with client manually to confirm they received the invite — kills adoption rate immediately

The 48-Hour Rule

Your target: both parents active within 48 hours of the attorney sending one invitation. That's the benchmark that makes attorneys say "this actually worked." If it takes longer than 48 hours, or if either parent needs help from the attorney to get set up, the flow needs work before you run another demo. Test this end-to-end with a real case before your first pilot attorney sends their first invitation.

Attorneys are tight-knit within their local bar. When one attorney finds something that works, they talk about it. Your job is to make that conversation as easy as possible and give champions a reason to have it. A well-structured referral program turns 10 anchor attorneys into 30 without a single cold email.

★

The bar association moment: Your highest-ROI marketing activity is getting one satisfied anchor attorney to mention CommonGround at a bar association meeting or CLE event. That single mention, in front of 30–50 family law attorneys, is worth 6 months of cold email. Make it easy for them to do this by giving them something specific to say.

  • Create the "request an attorney" moment — families on grant access who need legal help can be surfaced to your attorney directory
  • Start nonprofit outreach in Month 3–4, not Month 1 — after you have attorney density to make the connection meaningful
